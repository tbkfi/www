As Europe and its partners prepare to counter disinformation and hybrid warfare,
one glaring weakness remains: the public digital discourse lacks a verifiable chain
of trust. In the current environment malicious actors enjoy infinite anonymity while
decent people pay the ever-increasing price for keeping it civil.

The need for Digital ID as an option on platforms has never been higher --not as a
mandate across the entire internet, but as an **opt-in feature** that active participants
can choose to adopt. A near sure-fire way to filter out the majority of those unwilling
to commit to common rules of decency and fairness in the shared spaces.


## The Failure in Unchecked Anonymity

The current state of online spaces is nothing short of a security and usability disaster.
By refusing to bar, action, or reveal trolls, malicious users, and unidentified agents
from public spaces, platforms effectively incentivise and enable them to operate with
effective impunity.

**Toxicity Rots Communities:** Hate speech, death threats, harassment, and relentless
toxicity --despite what some tough people on the internet claim-- isn't normal. These
anti-social behaviours have become the norm in many online spaces, and it makes
building genuine communities more difficult that it needs to be.

**Asymmetry of Consequence:** Bad actors don’t get punished; they just re-create
accounts with a clean slate. Good people are unrewarded for decency and treated no better
than the best-behaving trolls on their umpteenth fresh account. The system enables cheap
toxicity, while making civility and longevity a liability.

**Information Warfare:** Hostile players on the world stage exploit this chaos daily.
They use anonymity to amplify disinformation, destabilise communities, and poison
discourse; and, without identification, it's impossible to distinguish between genuine
people and these foreign propaganda machines designed to divide people from the inside out.


## The Defensive Tool

Digital ID is not meant to be about surveillance; it's about **accountability** and
**user curated consistency**. It gives people the tools and options they currently lack
to take full responsibility for their digital lives and interactions:

**Protection for Good Actors:** Verified spaces allow people to interact securely
with those they want to, without fear of botnets or contextually subversive actors
muddying the waters or instigating needlessly divisive rhetoric.

**Community Curation:** Platforms and communities need robust mechanisms to filter
out toxicity for those who desire it. Real identity enables reputation systems where good
behavior is rewarded and abuse carries lasting consequences that can't be nullified
immediately with a fresh account and the clean slate it provides.

This isn't just about "nicer" social media, it's also a matter of national resilience.
Removing anonymity for bad actors from trusted spaces would obliterate much of the
adversaries' ability to project their strategic goals via information warfare.


## Preventing Abuse

Strong Digital ID is a devastatingly powerful tool. If implemented poorly as a system
or technically, it will be abused. Blind reliance on the current political leadership
--whose incompetence and lack of technical proficiency are well-documented-- is not going
to work. Their track record suggests that without strict boundaries and consideration in
implementation, the system will be expanded without restriction almost exclusively for
surveillance to the benefit of the few who deem themselves above it.

To prevent this system from being weaponised into mass surveillance infrastructure against
the citizens, its security should be tied directly to an uncompromisingly rigorous
cryptographic architecture and distributed design principle, rather than some vague policy
promises.


### Distributed Trust

Sovereignty must not be yielded to a single fallible entity or
a monolithic database (e.g. a centralised EU-wide registry). Centralisation makes systems
vulnerable to compromise by hostile states, corrupt insiders, or populists of the decade.
Intertwined, independent, cross-interfacing chains of authenticity are the golden standard
for longevity. The failure or corruption of one node musn't cascade into total systemic control.

### Selective Disclosure and Privacy-Preserving Protocols
The system must allow users to verify required attributes selectively (e.g., human, name,
18+, location, etc.) without revealing their full identity or facilitating cross-platform
tracking. Relying on opaque, proprietary implementations is simply unacceptable. THe standards
must be open and support **zero-knowledge proofs and minimal data exposure by default**.

### Limited Scope
It should never become mandatory across the entire internet, nor should it flagrantly violate
citizens rights to basic privacy by permeating private messaging channels. Outlawing encryption
is something that only a bonafide dumbass would seriously recommend, and if someone does:
just ask them, is XOR:ing now banned --and how?

### Unwavering Transparency
Any entity managing the core protocols of system this critical must operate with verifiable
transparency. This means open-source auditing for the whole pipeline. If a leader or
organisation refuses to subject their infrastructure and operations to public scrutiny
--or exempts themselves from the same rules they mandate for others-- their legitimacy is lost
forever.


## Conclusion

We are approaching a tipping point where Digital ID has become a morally valid, and functionally necessary,
option to offer for people in managing the various digital-space threats they may face.
If built correctly --rooted in sound engineering rather than politically convenient red herrings--
it's possible to stop bad actors from dictating the rules while decent people are forced to sit on their hands.
